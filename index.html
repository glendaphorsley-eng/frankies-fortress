A quiet way to surround a family with support.

Start a FortressSee an example

When a family is going through a mental health or substance use crisis, or grieving a loss, shame often keeps them from asking for help. Frankie's Fortress lets the people who love them show up with meals, rides and kindness, without anyone needing to explain.

How it works

  1. Build a Fortress

    The family, or a friend on their behalf, creates a private page and chooses the kinds of help they'd welcome.

  2. Share it quietly

    Send the private link only to people you trust. It never appears in searches or public lists.

  3. Friends show up

    Helpers sign up for meals, rides, errands or a simple check-in text. Nobody asks for details.

Privacy comes first

  • No explanation needed. There's no place to describe a diagnosis or what happened, ever.
  • Private link, with an optional passcode.
  • The address is shared only with someone who signs up to drop something off.
  • No-contact help: leave it at the door, no need to answer or reply.
  • Helpers can stay private too, if the family prefers.

For hard seasons, and for grief

A Fortress can support a family during an active crisis, or after they've lost someone they love to suicide, overdose or illness. Grief after these losses is often lonely. Steady, quiet support helps more than people know.

How to support a family without making it harder

Families facing mental illness, addiction or a loss like suicide or overdose often carry shame that isn't theirs to carry. The kindest help is steady, practical and asks for nothing back.

Please do
  • Offer something specific: "I'm bringing dinner Tuesday."
  • Keep showing up for months, not just the first weeks.
  • Send messages that need no reply.
  • Remember hard dates, like birthdays and anniversaries.
  • After a loss, say their loved one's name, if the family does.
  • Keep what you know private.
Please don't
  • Ask what happened, or for any details.
  • Give advice, opinions or stories about other people.
  • Say "everything happens for a reason" or "at least…"
  • Share their situation with others.
  • Take silence personally. They may not have words yet.
